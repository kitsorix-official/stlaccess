What Scale Are D&D Minis?

D&D miniatures use 28mm scale. A standard Medium humanoid measures 28mm to eye level, based on a 160cm eye-level height (175cm total) at 1:56 ratio. This is the baseline for all D&D miniature sizes.

D&D Miniature Size Chart

D&D 5e uses six size categories. Each category has a height range and corresponding miniature size in millimeters:

  • Tiny (under 2.5ft / 0.76m): 12-14mm — cats, imps, sprites
  • Small (2.5-5ft / 0.76-1.5m): 21-28mm — halflings, goblins, dwarves
  • Medium (5-10ft / 1.5-3m): 28-56mm — humans, elves, orcs
  • Large (10-20ft / 3-6m): 56-112mm — ogres, horses, trolls
  • Huge (20-40ft / 6-12m): 112-224mm — hill giants, young dragons
  • Gargantuan (40ft+ / 12m+): 224mm+ — ancient dragons, tarrasque

How to Scale D&D Miniatures

To convert between D&D size categories, use these percentages in your slicer:

  • Small to Medium: 133% (21mm to 28mm)
  • Medium to Large: 200% (28mm to 56mm)
  • Medium to Huge: 400% (28mm to 112mm)
  • Medium to Gargantuan: 800% (28mm to 224mm)

Use uniform scaling across all axes. Enter the percentage in your slicer's scale settings.

Standard D&D Mini Size

The standard D&D mini size for a Medium humanoid is 28mm to eye level. This is the size you get from official Wizards of the Coast miniatures and most 28mm STL files. If your mini measures 28mm from base to eye level, it is correctly scaled for D&D.

FDM Printing Tips for D&D Miniatures

Use a 0.2mm nozzle at 0.06mm layer height for humanoid faces. Print at 20-30mm/s for outer walls. Orient the face upward to minimize visible layer lines on the most important detail area.
