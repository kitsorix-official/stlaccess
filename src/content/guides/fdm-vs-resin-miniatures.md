There is no single right answer to resin versus FDM for miniatures. I printed the same model on both, painted them side by side, and the result was closer than I expected — and more specific than any blanket "one is better" answer.

Resin wins for tiny, delicate detail and easy support removal. FDM wins for speed, cost, and safety. Once painted and on the table at arm's length, both look great; the gap mostly shows up close in the hand. The real question is which models you print.

## The Head-to-Head

| Factor | Resin | FDM |
|---|---|---|
| Fine detail | Excellent — handles tiny, fragile contrast details | Good, sharpest with a 0.2mm nozzle |
| Support removal | Snaps off almost cleanly | Needs patience and cutting, more scarring |
| Speed | Slower per print | Faster, especially with coarse supports |
| Cost | Higher (resin, wash, cleanup) | Lower, PLA is cheap |
| Safety | Toxic fumes + skin contact, needs care | PLA is basically safe |
| Order of print | Beeper, more cleanup mess | Simpler to run |

The single biggest practical gap is support removal. Resin supports are thin ladders that come off with a gentle pull and leave almost nothing behind. FDM supports are thicker, can fuse to the model, and removing them is where most beginners break a delicate piece. This is the reason troubleshooting FDM supports is worth your time — it closes most of the quality gap.

## Where Resin Clearly Wins

For models with fine, fragile geometry, resin is the right call. Think a wizard's staff with a delicate finial, a model with a thin ornamental tail, or fine facial sculpting. Those features print cleaner in resin and hold up better when you handle them.

Resin also nails the very smallest detail at 28mm and below, where FDM layer lines start to compete with the sculpt. If you hold your miniatures up in your hand and pore over them, resin is what you want.

## Where FDM Clearly Wins

FDM is the pragmatic choice for most wargaming armies. It's faster, cheaper, and you can run it in your house without a dedicated, ventilated setup. PLA filament is a fraction of the cost of resin plus wash and cleanup supplies.

It's also genuinely fine for the models most people actually print. Big, angular armor plates read great on FDM — a Space Marine or a mech has large flat surfaces where layers just look like part of the build. Monsters and vehicles with chunky geometry do well too.

## Painted and on the Table, They Meet in the Middle

Here's what surprised me: after a few coats of paint and viewed from across the table, the two prints from my comparison were nearly indistinguishable in a miniatures comparison. At arm's length, both read as proper minis. It's only when you bring them close and study the fine details that the resin one separates itself.

Primer closes a lot of the gap on FDM. A coat of filler primer fills the micro-gaps between layers and makes FDM minis read as store-bought plastic. That one step turns "layered" into "painted."

## Which Should You Print?

Use this as a rough rule of thumb:

- **Resin:** tiny, delicate, or extremely detailed models — swords, tails, fine faces, display pieces you'll pore over.
- **FDM:** rank-and-file infantry, vehicles, terrain, monsters with chunky geometry — especially when you need quantity and speed.
- **Either:** most standard wargaming models, once you account for the support and printing quirks of each.

If you already own an FDM printer, don't feel you need to rush out and buy a resin one. Start with a 0.2mm nozzle and good settings, and only reach for resin if the fine detail you want is genuinely beyond FDM.

## Summary

- Resin wins on fine, fragile detail and support removal; costs more and needs safety care.
- FDM wins on speed, cost, and safety; fine on angular and chunky models.
- Painted and at table distance, both look great — the gap is up close.
- Pick by the model, not the hype.

The truth is most armies don't need resin. If you're printing quantities of rocky infantry and big machines, an FDM printer with a 0.2mm nozzle is enough. Save resin for the delicate heroes and display pieces that genuinely benefit from it.
